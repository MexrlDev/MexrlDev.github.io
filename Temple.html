Terry A. Davis  ·  1969 – 2018
Terry Andrew Davis began building TempleOS around 2007, after years working as a programmer at Ticketmaster. A period of profound mental crisis — later diagnosed as schizophrenia — led him to believe he was receiving direct messages from God. He left his job, moved into his parents’ house, and began writing an entire operating system from nothing. He called it LoseThos first, then J Operating System, and finally TempleOS. The 64-bit OS — kernel, compiler, graphics layer, and HolyC language — was written by one person. He designed it as “God’s third temple”, a sacred space for communion through code. Everything ran at 640×480, sixteen colours, through a custom VGA driver. Scripture and raw commentary ran through the codebase the way veins run through stone. By 2013 his online presence had grown into thousands of hours of video: livestreamed coding sessions, furious rants, moments of devastating clarity. For several years he was homeless, working out of his van on a battered ThinkPad, still refining the OS. The programming community slowly recognised what it was looking at — a self-hosted compiler, a full kernel, a 2D/3D graphics engine, no external libraries, no team. One man. On 11 August 2018, Terry was struck by a train in The Dalles, Oregon. He was 48. TempleOS is exactly as he left it.
